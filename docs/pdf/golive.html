Your prices are not approved, so the assistant will not quote. It collects every detail, tells the customer a member of your team will confirm the figure, and puts the enquiry in Escalations with everything you need. That is deliberate — the rates in the system now are placeholders derived from typical London market rates, not yours.

Task 2 fixes this, and it is the single most valuable thing on the list.

Part 2 — Everything left for you to do

In the order I would do them. The first three are the ones that matter; the rest can follow at your own pace.

Do first · legally required — 10 minutes, £52/year

1. Register with the ICO

Any UK organisation that handles personal data must register and pay the data protection fee. You are storing customers' names, addresses and phone numbers, so this is not optional and it carries a fixed penalty if skipped.

You are Tier 1 (micro): up to 10 staff or £632,000 turnover. £52/year, or £47 by direct debit.

Go to: ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee

Then tell me the registration number and I will put it in the privacy notice, which currently has a placeholder.

Do first · unlocks quoting — 30–60 minutes, free

2. Enter and approve your real prices

Open Dashboard → Pricing. Go through every figure and replace it with yours: base charge, price per bedroom or per 1,000 sq ft, minimum charge, the range spread, and hours on site, for each of the three services. Then the add-ons (oven, carpets, windows, same-day, weekend), then the London zone multipliers.

When you are happy, tick the approval box at the top. Until that tick, no price ever reaches a customer.

The page shows you a worked example as you edit, so you can check what a customer would actually be told before you commit.

Why this matters more than anything else

Right now every quote request becomes an escalation you answer by hand. Approve the rates and the assistant quotes instantly, at two in the morning, without you. That is most of the value of the whole system.

Do first · the assistant is currently silent on these — 20 minutes, free

3. Decide your cancellation and payment terms

Two things the assistant refuses to answer because nobody has told it the answer: what your cancellation policy is and when payment is due. Customers ask both regularly, and each one currently becomes an escalation.

Send me your answers in plain English — for example "free cancellation up to 24 hours before, half the fee after that" and "payment on completion, card or bank transfer" — and I will put them into the knowledge base and mark them approved.

Also worth settling while you are at it: how many staff you have and how long you have been trading. Both are currently marked unapproved, so the assistant will not state them.

Before launch — 15 minutes, £20/month

4. Move hosting to Vercel Pro

The site is on Vercel's free Hobby plan, which the company's own terms describe as for personal, non-commercial use. That has been correct for building and testing. From the day merrycleaners.co.uk points at it and real enquiries arrive, it is commercial use and Pro is the compliant plan.

$20/month. Upgrade at vercel.com/pricing — it is a plan change on the account that already holds the site, not a redeployment.

Before launch — 20 minutes, from $5/month

5. Add an Anthropic API key

Today the assistant answers from written rules. They are careful and they never invent anything, but they cannot handle a question phrased unusually. With a key it becomes genuinely conversational — and every guardrail, price rule and booking rule stays exactly as it is, because those run before the model is ever consulted.

Create a key at console.anthropic.com, add credit (start with $5), then paste the key into Vercel as ANTHROPIC_API_KEY. Expect roughly $5–15/month at this volume.

Test it afterwards in Dashboard → Mock mode, which runs rehearsal conversations through the real assistant without touching a real customer.

Before launch — 30 minutes, free

6. Connect email sending

Create a free account at resend.com, verify merrycleaners.co.uk by adding the DNS records it gives you, then paste the API key into Vercel as RESEND_API_KEY.

Free tier: 3,000 emails a month, 100 a day. That covers several hundred enquiries — you would need roughly a thousand a month before paying.

Until this is done, replies are drafted and held in Escalations but never sent.

When you want to take deposits — 45 minutes, pay per transaction

7. Connect Stripe

Only needed if you want customers to pay a deposit or the full amount online. Create an account at stripe.com/gb, complete their identity checks, and paste the secret key into Vercel as STRIPE_SECRET_KEY.

No monthly fee. 1.5% + 20p per UK card payment. On a £200 end-of-tenancy clean that is about £3.20.

Before launch — 20 minutes, free

8. Point your domain at the new site

The site currently lives at merry-cleaners.vercel.app. When you are satisfied with it, add merrycleaners.co.uk as a domain in the Vercel dashboard and update the DNS records at whoever you bought the domain from.

Do this last

The moment DNS changes, this becomes the live site your customers see. Everything above should be done first — particularly approving your prices.

Worth doing · free — 10 minutes

9. Get a free Companies House API key

Improves the prospecting agent. With a key it confirms each organisation against the official register — that it exists, that it is still trading, what it actually does — which lifts a prospect from "medium" to "very high" confidence before you approach anyone.

Free, no card. Register at developer.company-information.service.gov.uk, then paste it into Vercel as COMPANIES_HOUSE_API_KEY.

Optional — 1–2 hours

10. WhatsApp Business (optional)

Only if you want the assistant answering on WhatsApp as well as the website. It needs a Meta Business account, a verified business, and a phone number that is not already on WhatsApp. Free for the first 1,000 service conversations a month.

My honest advice: leave this until the website side is earning. It is the most fiddly setup on this list and the least likely to change your revenue.

Within a few months — $25/month

11. Move Supabase to Pro

The database is on the free tier, which has no backups and pauses after a week of inactivity. That is fine while you are testing. Once real customer records are in it, the lack of backups is the risk — one bad day and there is no way back.

Not urgent on day one, but do not leave it a year. supabase.com/pricing

Ongoing — a few minutes a week

12. Work the prospect list

There are already 28 real London agencies on file, found in public records. Nobody has been contacted. Open Dashboard → Prospects, read a record, check the draft approach it has written, and send it yourself if you are happy with it.

The system deliberately will not send anything for you, and cannot send in bulk. Twenty considered approaches will beat a thousand automated ones, and it keeps your domain out of spam filters.

Part 4 — Security

The system was reviewed twice, the second time as a deliberate attempt to break it. Seven weaknesses were found and all seven are fixed and covered by tests, so if one is ever undone the test suite fails.

What was wrongWhat it would have allowedNow
The server would fetch any address given to it Prospect websites come from OpenStreetMap, which anyone can edit. A hostile entry could have made your server read its own internal systems and display the result in your dashboard. Fixed
A guessable key could sign a dashboard session If a setting were ever missing, someone could forge a sign-in without knowing your password. Fixed — it now locks instead
Scheduled jobs allowed anyone through if their password was unset Strangers triggering your automated runs, or posting fake customer emails into your inbox. Fixed — refuses instead
Passwords could be guessed without limit An automated attack on the one password protecting the dashboard. Fixed — 10 tries per 15 minutes
One visitor could read another customer's record Someone holding a customer reference could ask the assistant about that person and get their name, postcode and booking dates. Fixed
The browser had no security instructions An injected script would have run; your booking form could be hidden inside another site. Fixed
Internal error text was shown to visitors Leaked details about how the system is built. Fixed

Three things that stay your responsibility

  • Use a strong dashboard password and do not reuse it anywhere else. One password protects every customer record. A long phrase of four unrelated words beats a short complicated one.
  • Never paste an API key into a chat, an email or a document. They go into the Vercel environment variables and nowhere else. Anyone holding your Anthropic key can spend your money; anyone holding your Supabase key can read every customer record.
  • Sign out on a shared or public computer. The dashboard is never cached or indexed, but a session lasts seven days on the machine you used.

Also worth knowing

The database has row-level security switched on with no public access rules at all, so it can only be reached through the server's own key — there is no public route to your customer data. Dependencies were audited: zero known vulnerabilities across eight runtime packages.

Reading order, if you only do three things

  1. Register with the ICO — it is a legal requirement and takes ten minutes.
  2. Approve your prices — it is what turns the assistant from a message-taker into something that sells.
  3. Upgrade Vercel to Pro — before real customers arrive, because the free plan is not licensed for commercial use.

Everything else can wait, and nothing else is blocking you.
